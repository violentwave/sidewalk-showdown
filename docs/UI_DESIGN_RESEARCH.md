# Make Tripp's Stream the System, Not the Skin

Sidewalk Showdown's menus and HUD gain real depth by turning the planned "cheap livestream overlay" into a small deterministic audience model: a few integers (viewers, hype, sentiment, exposure) fed by sim events the game already produces, rendered through a joke layer that evolves across the campaign, while the "arcade cabinet" layer stays fixed, framed, and honest. The research supports this split directly. Guilty Gear Strive shows what happens when style moves or dims critical information: its director admitted the first UI "simply failed" after **80% of beta feedback concerned the UI** ([Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2020/02/07/daisuke-ishiwatari-from-arc-system-works-states-that-the-team-failed-with-the-current-ui-of-guilty-gear-strive-currently-working-on-new-ui/)). Meanwhile, the most convincing parody interfaces (Needy Streamer Overload, Hypnospace Outlaw, Not For Broadcast) work because real numbers and real state sit behind the fake screen. On top of that model, four tools add the "uniqueness" layer cheaply for a solo developer: Ultrakill-style named rank escalation, Balatro-style sequenced juice, Pizza Tower-style reactive host faces, and a stem-based audio identity voiced by Tripp. The biggest open decision is Logan's: whether the audience state lives inside the serialized simulation (cosmetic-only but replay-exact) or stays pure presentation derived from confirmed events. This report separates **sourced findings** (cited) from **design proposals** (labelled as such). Items marked UNVERIFIED come from the researchers' recollection and should not drive decisions until checked.

A note on evidence quality before the details: the research notes verified a narrow slice of the requested games. Strive, SF6's HUD skins, Needy Streamer Overload, Hypnospace Outlaw, Not For Broadcast, Def Jam: Fight for NY, Smash's crowd, Punch-Out!!, Spec Ops, Ultrakill, Pizza Tower, Persona/Metaphor, Cuphead, and (via third-party analysis) Balatro were verified. Skullgirls, 3rd Strike, Garou, KOF XV, Tekken 8, MK1, Streets of Rage 4, River City Girls, Scott Pilgrim, Sifu, Hi-Fi Rush, Hotline Miami, Jet Set Radio, Dead Space, Inscryption, Doki Doki Literature Club, and Pony Island were **not** verified. Where those games would be natural references, this report either omits them or flags them.

## Strive's failure sets the rules for the cabinet layer

The single best-documented lesson in the notes is a negative one. A detailed UX critique of Guilty Gear Strive against its predecessor Xrd found that Strive's **Burst gauge moves around the screen**, making a "life-or-death" resource hard to check; its tension meter lost Xrd's segmented, colour-coded design and kept only a small gear icon at 50%; its lighter pink/green palette lost contrast on snow and jungle stages; its bars **lost Xrd's metal frames**; and dust, explosions and zooms overlapped the bars ([uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/can-an-interface-condemn-a-title-guilty-gear-a-practical-approach-to-how-ui-impacts-gameplay-845895b7c70d)). The same critique notes an inverted hierarchy: Xrd's hit counter **changed colour to warn that an air combo had become escapable**, so it carried real information, while Strive's counter grows larger with every hit but conveys nothing actionable, so it is pure clutter ([uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/can-an-interface-condemn-a-title-guilty-gear-a-practical-approach-to-how-ui-impacts-gameplay-845895b7c70d)). At the menu level, a separate case study flagged joke-free but still confusing labels: a "recording settings" tab that does not record, a buried Quick Start, and a Quick Start flow that **reveals the opponent's character before acceptance**, which enables matchup-dodging. The critic recommends revealing it only on the loading/VS screen "as other fighting games do" ([PatternFly / Medium](https://medium.com/patternfly/fighting-games-ux-holds-the-genre-back-a-case-study-into-guilty-gear-strive-s-ux-e0c5e24eae0f)).

These findings validate the existing two-layer plan and sharpen it into rules. **Proposal (cabinet-layer contract):** health, ghost health, meter, timer, round pips and win letters never change position, never lose their frame, and never sit under VFX. Expression happens *in place*: colour swaps, palette-cycled pulses, 1-2 px integer shakes, and portrait frame swaps. At 320x180, Xrd's metal frame becomes a 1 px dark outline plus a 1 px light bevel. Every bar colour gets tested against the brightest and darkest regions of both Gas-N-Go and Bus Stop. Hit sparks and super flashes draw on a canvas layer *below* the HUD. Any counter on screen must earn its pixels the way Xrd's did; the planned callouts on the attacker's side should carry a state (counter-hit, punish, low-meter warning) rather than just grow.

The menu lesson is equally direct. **Proposal:** every themed label gets a plain-language sub-label ("REGULAR · Arcade", "PREMIUM · Versus", "DIESEL · Training"). The joke goes on top; the function sits underneath in the readable body font. The opponent's pick in versus or online stays hidden until Tripp's "TONIGHT'S MATCHUP" bumper. That serves anti-dodging and gives the bumper a reveal to perform.

One more verified precedent bears on the cabinet layer's long-term life. SF6's December 2025 update added **purchasable retro HUD skins** (Super SF2 Turbo, Alpha 3, 3rd Strike, USF4 with its graffiti font, SFV CE) plus a free "SimSim" skin based on the Battle Hub training robot ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2025/12/street-fighter-6s-new-battle-hud-setting-on-ps5-ps4-is-so-simple-yet-so-fun)). Reviewers liked the idea, but critics pointed out that the skins **do not re-theme round pips, portraits, round/K.O. text, the combo counter or notifications**, which made them read as cheap ([DashFight](https://dashfight.com/news/street-fighter-6-new-battle-ui-update-leaves-a-lot-to-be-desired-8231)). **Proposal:** if Sidewalk Showdown ever offers HUD themes, theme the *joke layer only* (stream overlays as unlockables) and theme every element of it together. The cabinet layer stays constant so readability never varies with cosmetics.

## A four-number audience model makes the overlay systemic

The strongest parody interfaces in the notes are machines with a costume on. Needy Streamer Overload's follower gain is a **deterministic formula**: a base of 0.01 × followers under 10k, or 25 × log10(followers) above, times a hidden per-stream multiplier, times bonuses such as streak and a pre-stream alert (×1.2), with a "Rabbit Hole" penalty of 1% per point. Its chat moderation is a mechanic, and at peak-intensity streams the chat **greys out with "We're not reading comments for this stream"** ([NSO wiki](https://needystreameroverload.wiki.gg/wiki/Stream)). That locked chat is a near-free story beat. Not every element needs to matter, though: NSO's superchats are flavour text with no gameplay effect ([NSO wiki](https://needystreameroverload.wiki.gg/wiki/Stream)). Fighting games have used audiences in three verified ways. Smash's crowd is pure feedback, with per-character chants and gasps on near-ledge survival ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Crowd)). Def Jam: Fight for NY's crowd is a resource, where momentum from landed moves, counters and taunts, scaled by Charisma, unlocks a "Blazin'" state ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Jam:_Fight_for_NY)). Def Jam's crowd is also a physical hazard that shoves and holds fighters ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Jam:_Fight_for_NY)). Smash also shows the power of absence: the crowd **goes silent in Melee's Sudden Death and disappears in climactic single-player fights** ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Crowd)).

Real streaming culture supplies a ready-made vocabulary. Twitch's native alerts cover follows, subs, interactive resubs, raids, goals, charity milestones and Hype Train events (start, level-up, record broken), with "Celebrations" such as emote rain attachable to any alert ([Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2023/07/25/celebrate-your-community-your-way-with-twitch-s-alerts)). The Hype Train is itself a threshold system: it kicks off when **3 to 25 support events land within a 5-minute window**, climbs through levels, and awards "Conductor" badges to top contributors ([StreamScheme](https://www.streamscheme.com/twitch-hype-train-complete-guide/)).

### Proposal: the Tripp audience model

The design below synthesises those precedents into something a solo developer can build in about a week of focused work. It has three parts: an event bus, a tiny state struct, and a reaction table.

The **event bus** emits frame-stamped events the sim already knows: hit landed, combo length reached N, counter-hit, throw, knockdown, near-death (HP under a threshold), comeback (HP lead flips), perfect round, timeout, turtling (no attacks for N frames, or a high block ratio), round win, KO, and taunt (if taunts exist).

The **state** is four integers. `viewers` is a big, jittery, mostly fake number. `hype` runs 0-100 and decays per frame, faster at higher ranks. `sentiment` runs -100 to +100 and tracks whether chat favours Tripp or the fighters. `exposure` is a campaign-level value that only moves between matches. All updates use integer maths. Chat-line variety comes from a hash of (match seed, frame, event id), never global RNG, so replays reproduce the same chat.

The **reaction table** maps events to joke-layer output:

| Sim event | Hype / viewers | Joke-layer reaction | Tripp (webcam face + bark) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combo ≥ threshold | hype spike; "HYPE TRAIN" level-up | "W" / emote burst in chat | Hype face, "CLIP THAT" |
| Counter-hit / punish | small spike | Callout echoed as an alert banner | Smirk |
| Near-death survival / comeback | viewers tick up | Chat floods "?????" (Melee-gasp analogue) | Shocked face |
| Perfect round | big spike | Emote rain; a sponsor bug "takes credit" | Self-congratulation |
| Timeout / turtling | viewers drain; sentiment toward Tripp | "L", "refund", "boring" | Mocking bark |
| Long low-hype stretch | — | Between rounds only: Tripp cuts to a sponsor read | Bored face |
| KO | settle | LIVE badge flips to "CLIP IT" | Round-end line |

Hype draws from landed hits, the same source the meter uses. That keeps it consistent with the project's meter rule as long as **it never feeds meter, damage or any combat value**. Ultrakill supplies the escalation grammar. It has eight named, colour-coded ranks from DESTRUCTIVE (200) to ULTRAKILL (1500); **decay multiplies up to 8× at the top rank**; overused bonuses **turn grey and award less**; and freshness runs from Fresh 1.5× to Dull 0× ([Ultrakill Wiki](https://ultrakill.fandom.com/wiki/Style_Index)). **Proposal:** a "stream rank" ladder written in the game's Florida voice. The researchers' placeholders are LURKERS → CLIPPABLE → TRENDING → WORLDSTAR → FLORIDA MAN; final names and tone are Logan's design-taste call. The ladder adds a freshness rule in which spamming one move greys the chat with "chat is bored". That gives the joke layer a reason to reward variety without touching balance.

### Where the state lives is Logan's decision

Two implementations satisfy the determinism rules, and they trade off differently. In **Option A (sim-resident, recommended)**, the four integers live in a small struct inside the match state, update in the 60 Hz tick from sim events, serialize through `MatchRoot.serialize()/load()`, and are read-only to combat code. Replays and rollback then reproduce the overlay exactly, and an online opponent sees the same chat. The cost is a few bytes of new sim state, which needs tests and Logan's sign-off because it touches the simulation. In **Option B (presentation-only)**, the overlay derives its state from the event log and reacts only to *confirmed* (post-rollback) events. Combat code is never touched, but online the overlay lags slightly, and replay fidelity depends on rebuilding from the log. The notes disagree slightly here: one researcher favours serializing, while another frames the overlay as purely reading sim state. Both are valid. Option A is simpler to make correct once rollback lands. Neither option should let the stream layer write to combat. **Any gameplay effect from the audience** (Def Jam-style crowd hazards, chat-voted stage events, a donation alert that stuns) is a balance decision for Logan. If he wants one, the safe home for it is the Tripp boss fight only.

## The overlay should rot as the scam unravels

Static jokes go stale; an overlay that changes across the campaign tells the story without cutscenes. The verified precedents are cheap. Spec Ops: The Line's loading screens begin as play tips, **"slowly devolve into philosophical questions,"** and end by questioning the player directly ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/features/loading-screens-spec-ops-line-chilling/)). That is an arc built purely by swapping string pools. Hypnospace Outlaw keeps the same Windows-9x-style interface across dated sessions while the world inside it changes: a buggy alpha bricks headbands, the "Mindcrash" shuts the network down, and a present-day archive uncovers corporate foreknowledge ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Hypnospace_Outlaw)). Not For Broadcast conveys story stress through **equipment malfunction (electrified controls, overheating)**. Its director's rule fits a 320x180 screen exactly: "At all times we put the needs of the game and the player over that of realism," and "Any time we could remove complexity, we did" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-2023-notgames-not-for-broadcast)). Punch-Out!!'s Title Defense remixes every known opponent with new tricks, such as King Hippo's manhole-cover guard, and tightens the knockdown limit from 4 to 3 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch-Out!!_(2009_video_game))). The same game turns an opponent's grin or taunt into a counter window that awards a Star ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch-Out!!_(2009_video_game))).

A framework point justifies all of this. In the Fagerholt and Lorentzon taxonomy, *meta* UI lives on the screen plane but supports the narrative, and fictional framing makes overlays plausible: Syndicate's DART overlay "works" because the setting justifies it ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/user-interface-design-in-video-games)). Tripp's overlay is meta UI that is *literally in the fiction*, since he really is streaming. That means it can lie, censor, advertise and collapse. The cabinet layer is non-diegetic and must do none of those things.

### Proposal: a five-state overlay arc keyed to campaign progress

| Overlay state | When | What changes (joke layer only) | Primary assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Polished hype | Early arcade stages | Clean LIVE bug, huge viewer count, confident lower-third ("TRIPP VLOGS PRESENTS: SIDEWALK SHOWDOWN $100K"), filling **PRIZE POOL** goal bar | Base overlay kit |
| 2. Cracks | Mid campaign | Chat asks "where's the prize money?"; visible "[message deleted]" moderation; sponsors swap to sketchier brands; viewer count jitters; a "technical difficulties" card between rounds | String pool swap, 2-3 sponsor sprites |
| 3. Unravel | Late campaign | Prize bar exposed as a static image or goes negative; lower-thirds get typos and corrections; chat locked (NSO-style "not reading comments" / followers-only); overlay glitches harder on big hits | Glitch shader params, locked-chat sprite |
| 4. Boss: overlay as arena | Tripp fight | Overlay pieces are knocked loose by hits on Tripp (sponsor bugs fall, LIVE flickers, viewers crash); Smash-style silence as the fake crowd drains to "0 viewers"; Tripp's self-promo popups become Punch-Out!!-style tells (and a punish window only if Logan approves) | Bespoke boss overlay |
| 5. Post-game | Save flag after ending | Title/attract mode changes: Tripp's channel reads "This channel is unavailable"; the fighters' own scrappy stream overlay replaces his | One title-screen variant |

The arc pairs with an escalation rule that keeps jokes fresh. The same event reads differently by state. In act 1 Tripp hypes a big combo; in act 3 he claims it was staged. **Proposal (string system):** each event type gets a line pool with a cooldown (no repeat within K events), a rare tier at roughly 5% chosen by deterministic hash, and context tags (matchup, stage, overlay state, player habit such as "this guy only throws"). No sourced postmortem on joke fatigue was found, so these numbers are a starting guess to tune in playtests. The "top donor" from the Hype Train's Conductor badge becomes a running results-screen joke that pays off when the donors turn out to be bots or Tripp himself, which is the scam made visible on a screen the player sees every match.

Pizza Tower sets a hard limit here. Its TV HUD was planned as a **"wisecracking host" with text bubbles; the feature was cut because players busy exploring didn't read the messages**. It was replaced by face sprites and one-time prompts ([TCRF](https://new.tcrf.net/Pizza_Tower/TV_Text_Bubble)). **Proposal:** Tripp's webcam box reacts with expression swaps and 1-3 word bursts during play. Full sentences go only between rounds, on the VS bumper, and on results. Interruptions such as sponsor reads, ads and "technical difficulties" happen only at round boundaries, never mid-exchange, and never cover the playfield. A setting to minimise or hide the stream layer covers accessibility and players who want pure arcade.

## Each screen becomes a bespoke object with one system inside

Atlus is the verified benchmark for menu identity, and it is also a warning about cost. Director Katsura Hashino says each Persona/Metaphor menu is **"a whole separate program running and a separate design,"** and admits "This is actually really annoying to do" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/-metaphor-refantazio-persona-5-menu-ui-hashino-1851667088)). He argues that treating UI as secondary to characters and backgrounds is "a mistake," which is "why Atlus' UI designers are paid so well" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/persona-3-reloads-ui-goes-hard-and-one-indie-dev-reckons-theyve-figured-out-why)). The art director keys each game's interface to **one colour (teal for P3, yellow for P4, crimson for P5)**, fills graphics by hand with no gradients, and iterated P5's black/white text until it read ([SEGAbits / Famitsu](https://segabits.com/?p=54918)). P5's angular boxes started out hard to read and took extensive iteration to fix ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/-metaphor-refantazio-persona-5-menu-ui-hashino-1851667088)). Cuphead built custom fonts from 1930s comic and title-card lettering and laid real scanned film grain over every frame ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/how-i-cuphead-i-nailed-the-look-and-feel-of-classic-1930s-cartoons)).

For a solo developer, the transferable version is: **one key colour, flat hand-filled shapes, bespoke treatment for about four high-traffic screens, and one shared component kit for everything else.** The key colour (gas-sign red, hot-pink neon, or something else) is Logan's taste call. The proposals below deepen each planned screen by giving it *one* system rather than more decoration.

**Main menu: Gas-N-Go price board.** Menu items are fuel grades with plain sub-labels, and changing selection clacks split-flap digits. *Depth:* the "prices" are live stats, such as total matches played, Tripp's current fake prize pool, and days since the stream's last "incident". They update with campaign progress and the overlay state, so the title screen itself rots across the arc (stage 5 above). The chat strip shows the overlay state's string pool, letting returning players see the story's progress before they press anything.

**Character select: mugshot board.** Hover plays a per-character bark and portrait animation, the cheapest high-personality element in the notes' juice checklist. Lock-in stamps "BOOKED" with a brief UI hit-stop. *Depth:* each placard carries a "charge" line that doubles as flavour and an indirect playstyle hint ("Assault w/ prosthetic"). Chat in the margin reacts to the pick with matchup-aware lines from the same audience pools. Two-player select needs per-device cursors, because Godot's single global focus cannot serve two players. Hidden-input traditions (hold a button for an alternate palette) fit the mugshot fiction as "alias" photos; specific genre precedents were UNVERIFIED in the notes.

**Stage select: scratch-off ticket.** Scratching reveals stages with a coin-scratch loop and a reveal chime. *Depth:* the ticket has a "fine print" row that changes by overlay state, and late in the campaign it reads as the literal scam terms. A random-stage option is a "mystery scratch", resolved by deterministic hash.

**VS bumper: "TONIGHT'S MATCHUP".** This is the reveal moment, per the Strive anti-dodging finding. *Depth:* Tripp writes a clickbait caption per matchup pair ("YOU WON'T BELIEVE WHO SHOWED UP"), with an insult line per pair written into a data file so the 13 planned fighters scale without code changes. The caption tone shifts with overlay state. Confirm skips it.

**Results: "Tripp's clip of the match".** Balatro's verified juice comes from *sequencing*: cards flip with a spring bounce, digits roll like slot reels, each scoring step plays a **rising pitch**, and screen shake scales with score size ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro); third-party analysis, not LocalThunk's documentation). *Depth:* the results tally stream stats line by line (damage, best combo, peak viewers, "donations", top donor) with rising pitch and rolling digits. Peak viewers acts as a soft arcade score, like Melee's crowd-based Bonus awards ("Crowd Favorite", "Master of Suspense", 2,500 points each) ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Crowd)). Rematch stays near-instant: one flash plus a "STREAM CONTINUES" stinger, with no full transition.

**Settings: Stream tab.** This holds overlay visibility (Full / Minimal / Off), chat density, Tripp bark frequency, and "Screen FX: Off / Low / Full". The accessibility criteria the Family Gaming Database tracks ("No Screen Shake", "No Busy Backgrounds", "Outline Interactive Elements", "Audio Cues for Visual Events", "Colour Blind Friendly") make a good checklist for the Accessibility tab ([Family Gaming Database](https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Balatro)). A reduced-flashing option matters for a mature fighter with hit flashes.

**Pause and loading text** (proposal): loading tips copy Spec Ops' arc. They start as useful tips, drift into Tripp's self-promotion, and end with the fighters' messages hijacking the tip slot.

## Motion and audio rules keep pixels honest and give Tripp a voice

### Motion language: whole pixels, sequenced, never on the input path

The pixel-craft findings are the most technical in the notes and should become hard rules. In Godot's `viewport` stretch mode the game renders at base resolution, so **"tweens and particle systems look jagged"**, while `canvas_items` allows smooth effects; **integer scale mode requires Godot 4.3+** ([GDQuest](https://www.gdquest.com/library/pixel_art_setup_godot4/)). The researcher's inference for this project is that motion must be authored in whole pixels whichever mode is used: round every tweened position, use eased integer sequences (a 12 px slide over 6 frames as 0, 5, 8, 10, 11, 12, where the last frames "settle" 1 px at a time), draw 2-3 squash frames instead of scaling nodes, never rotate pixel sprites except in 90° steps, and use **palette cycling and Bayer-dither wipes instead of alpha fades**, since alpha produces off-palette colours. Shakes use integer offsets of 1-3 px from a fixed sequence, decaying over 6-12 frames, with a toggle. Nijman's "Art of Screenshake" talk is the canonical evidence that **about 30 small layered tweaks** transform feel ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). The specific frame counts above are proposals, not sourced timings.

**Proposal (motion grammar):** each layer moves in its own way, so players learn by feel which layer is talking. The cabinet layer is *mechanical*: it flips, stamps and clacks, moves only in place, and never drifts. The stream layer is *digital and cheap*: it pops in with one-frame smears, stutters, buffers, and glitches with macroblock smears on big hits. Menu juice follows four verbs. Hover is a 1-2 px lift plus an outline swap within 1-3 frames. Confirm is a 3-6 frame UI hit-stop then a stamp. Back is a reverse wipe with a softer, lower sound. Idle is 2-frame loops such as neon flicker or a buzzing fly. The fighter-specific rule overrides everything: **register input first, animate after, and let every animation be skipped or buffered through.** Lavish transitions belong to non-repeating moments (boot, first VS, ending). Persona's 12-second day change works only because it is a breathing moment in an RPG ([itch.io study](https://itch.io/blog/533534/making-ui-come-to-life-study-of-persona-5)); a fighter's rematch loop gets near-zero. One shared transition shader with per-theme mask textures (price-flip, scratch, static burst, "technical difficulties") keeps cost to a single node.

**Post-processing** (proposal): two treatments for two layers. The cabinet layer gets a subtle CRT pass; a community "CRT with Luminance Preservation" shader avoids dimming ([Godot Shaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/crt-with-luminance-preservation/)). The stream layer gets compression blocks, chroma bleed and bitrate drops confined to its own frame art. The readable HUD sits on a higher CanvasLayer that the pass does not touch. Scanlines must be computed at output resolution, or they erase whole game-pixel rows. In practice that means rendering the game in a 320x180 SubViewport shown through an integer-scaled TextureRect and running the shader there. That is an architecture change an agent should prototype on a branch first.

### Fonts: pixel-native for information, baked sprites for shouts

The researcher notes that the project currently uses Bangers, Comic Neue and Share Tech Mono (from the notes; verify against the repo). Those are vector fonts that blur or mix pixel sizes at 320x180. Godot contributor Calinou states that **pixel fonts "must be used at their intended size (or integer multiples of that size)"** and that subpixel positioning must be disabled in the font's Import dock ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/why-pixel-font-blurry-godot-4/735)). The verified free options, with licences checked on their pages, are listed below.

| Font | Licence (as stated on source) | Proposed role | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| m5x7 (Daniel Linssen) | CC0; attribution appreciated, not required | HUD names/numbers, menu body | [itch.io](https://managore.itch.io/m5x7) |
| monogram (datagoblin) | CC0; nothing required; ships a bitmap PNG + JSON | Alternative body font; bitmap with baked outlines | [itch.io](https://datagoblin.itch.io/monogram) |
| Pixel Operator (Jayvee Enaguas) | CC0 (verified via DaFont mirror only) | Stream chat/ticker (Mono or 8 px variants) | [DaFont](https://www.dafont.com/pixel-operator.font?text=IT) |
| m6x11 (Daniel Linssen) | Free **with attribution required** | Chunky headers; Balatro's font | [itch.io](https://managore.itch.io/m6x11), [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/65816/balatro-computer-game) |

**Proposal:** information text uses one CC0 pixel font. Stream chat uses a second, "cheaper" monospace font so the layers read differently at a glance. Big shouts ("ROUND 1", "K.O.", "BOOKED") become hand-cleaned sprite lettering, which can include Bangers rasterised once at target size, never live TTF text. Each real-object screen may carry one vernacular display face (split-flap digits, hand-painted "COLD BEER" lettering, scratch-off foil type), following Cuphead's period-lettering logic. **Licensing is Logan's call**, especially m6x11's mandatory credit. Choosing between m5x7 and monogram is a taste test best done by screenshot at 1x. The researcher's capacity estimate is that m5x7 gives about 53 characters across and about 18 lines down. It suggests a top HUD band of about 20-24 px and a bottom band of about 12-16 px, leaving about 140 px of clear play space; this is inference to check against the existing HUD layout.

### Audio identity: one track with stems, two voices, one sound family

Audiokinetic's UI-audio guidance is the sourced foundation. **Ascending sounds signal success, entering and opening; descending sounds signal failure, leaving and closing.** Consonant reads positive and inharmonic negative. Pitch and asset randomisation fight fatigue. Failure can be signalled by *removing layers*. Negative sounds should never be harsh ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/approaching-ui-audio-ui-design-perspective-2)). SF6 treats commentary as an ongoing content line: its real-time commentary system ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/preview/2022/06/10/getting-a-feel-for-the-new-real-time-commentary-system)) gained a new commentator in December 2025 ([DashFight](https://dashfight.com/news/street-fighter-6-new-battle-ui-update-leaves-a-lot-to-be-desired-8231)). For placeholder or final SFX, **ChipTone output is CC0** ([itch.io](https://sfbgames.itch.io/chiptone)) and **jfxr output carries "no restrictions whatsoever"** ([GitHub](https://github.com/ttencate/jfxr)).

**Proposal (audio architecture):** there are two voices, matching the two layers. A neutral arcade announcer handles "ROUND 1 / FIGHT / K.O." so information audio stays clear. Tripp is the joke-layer voice, delivering commentator barks keyed to the reaction table, under 1.5 s on menus, drawn from shuffle bags with no immediate repeats. Music is one menu track split into stems (drums, bass, pad, lead), and screens add or remove layers so transitions never restart the song. The full mix plays on the title; character select adds percussion; the VS bumper plays a stinger into stage music. The music low-passes while a sub-menu is open. Diegetic UI sounds come per object: split-flap clacks for the price board, camera flash and booking stamp for mugshots, coin-scratch for the ticket, and original donation chimes and notification pops for the stream layer. Those stream sounds must be original, not sound-alikes of real platform alerts (a licensing caution). The stream rank drives music intensity and Tripp's bark tier. The results tally uses Balatro's rising pitch per line.

The Godot classes for this (`AudioStreamRandomizer`, `AudioStreamSynchronized`, and `AudioStreamInteractive` with beat-quantised transitions, 4.3+) come from the researcher's general knowledge. The official 4.7 docs could not be fetched, so an agent should confirm names and behaviour. Use separate buses (Music, SFX, UI, Voice) with Voice ducking Music. One determinism rule is critical: **in-match audio-reactive visuals must pulse from sim frame count at a known BPM, never from audio playback position**, which depends on wall-clock time.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "unique" should mean for this project. Uniqueness will not come from more ornament on the price board or mugshot board. It will come from a stream overlay that *knows things*: it tallies the match, remembers the campaign, and decays as Tripp's scam falls apart. That approach plays to Sidewalk Showdown's existing technical strengths. A deterministic event-driven sim makes a replay-exact fake audience nearly free, and few fighters could offer that, since their crowds are unseeded presentation. It also fits the narrative unusually well: the joke layer is the antagonist's instrument, so breaking it is both the story's climax and the overlay's final state. The most important discipline is the asymmetry. Everything about Tripp may lie, glitch, censor and collapse; nothing the player needs to win may move.

The evidence gaps are real and worth one targeted follow-up pass before committing art budget. The genre's best-known comeback-state HUDs (Tekken 8 Rage, SF6 Burnout, Garou's T.O.P. zone, Skullgirls' recoverable health), the beat-'em-up results screens (Streets of Rage 4, River City Girls' phone menu), and the corrupted-UI canon (Doki Doki, Pony Island, Inscryption) all went unverified. The quickest verification route is screenshots from the Game UI Database. That pass should check the escalation and campaign-arc proposals against how those games actually pace their reveals, not reopen the core architecture, which the verified sources already support.
